# Navagraha (the nine grahas)

### navagraha-overview
- topic: navagraha
- title: The Navagraha, nine grahas of Vedic astrology
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra
- lang: en

The Navagraha are the nine "grahas" (literally "those that seize" or influences) of Jyotish, Vedic astrology: Surya (Sun), Chandra (Moon), Mangal (Mars), Budh (Mercury), Guru or Brihaspati (Jupiter), Shukra (Venus), Shani (Saturn), and the two shadow points Rahu and Ketu. Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra is the classical text most widely cited for their significations. Seven of them give their names to the days of the week. Many Hindu temples have a Navagraha shrine, and devotees often circle it after the main deity. Colours, grains, metals and gems linked to each graha vary somewhat between traditions.

### navagraha-colours-summary
- topic: colour
- title: Commonly given colours for the nine grahas
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

In many panchang and temple traditions the grahas are linked to colours as follows: Surya with red, orange or saffron; Chandra with white or cream; Mangal with red; Budh with green; Guru with yellow; Shukra with white, cream or soft pastel shades; Shani with black or dark blue; Rahu with smoky or dark blue-grey; Ketu with grey, brown or multicoloured. Lists differ by region and family, and some priests give slightly different shades. These are customs for what to wear or offer on a given day, and devotees follow them according to their own family practice.

### surya-significations
- topic: navagraha
- title: Surya (Sun) in Vedic astrology
- graha: surya
- weekday: sunday
- deity: surya
- source: Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra
- lang: en

Surya, the Sun, is described in Jyotish as the "atma karaka", the significator of the soul or self, and is linked with vitality, authority, the father and leadership. Its day is Sunday (Ravivar). Commonly linked colours are red, orange and saffron; the metal is usually copper or gold; the grain is wheat; the gem is ruby (manik). Surya is worshipped as the visible deity of light. Interpretations differ among astrologers, and these associations describe what the tradition holds, not a prediction for any individual.

### surya-practices
- topic: navagraha
- title: Surya worship practices and beej mantra
- graha: surya
- weekday: sunday
- deity: surya
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Devotees traditionally offer water (arghya) to the rising Sun, often in a copper vessel, while reciting Surya mantras. The beej (seed) mantra commonly given is "Om Hraam Hreem Hraum Sah Suryaya Namah". Other well-known practices are Surya Namaskar, the Gayatri Mantra addressed to the Sun as Savitr, and reading the Aditya Hridayam, a hymn to Surya that appears in the Ramayana. Many families keep Sunday observances such as eating simply and avoiding salt, though custom varies.

### chandra-significations
- topic: navagraha
- title: Chandra (Moon) in Vedic astrology
- graha: chandra
- weekday: monday
- deity: shiva
- source: Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra
- lang: en

Chandra, the Moon, is linked in Jyotish with the mind (manas), emotions, mother and nourishment. Its day is Monday (Somvar). Commonly given colours are white and cream; the metal is silver; the grain is rice; the gem is pearl (moti). Shiva, who wears the crescent moon on his head, is the deity most often connected with Monday and the Moon. The Moon's phases are also the basis of the lunar calendar that sets many Hindu festivals. These are traditional associations, and astrologers differ on details.

### chandra-practices
- topic: navagraha
- title: Chandra worship practices and beej mantra
- graha: chandra
- weekday: monday
- deity: shiva
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Monday observances for Chandra overlap with Shiva worship: many devotees fast on Mondays (Somvar vrat), offer water or milk to the Shivling, and wear white. The beej mantra commonly given is "Om Shraam Shreem Shraum Sah Chandraya Namah". Purnima (full moon) and Pradosh evenings are also considered auspicious times for Moon and Shiva worship. Whether a person follows these customs depends on family tradition, and practices differ between regions.

### mangal-significations
- topic: navagraha
- title: Mangal (Mars) in Vedic astrology
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: hanuman
- source: Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra
- lang: en

Mangal, Mars, is linked in Jyotish with courage, energy, siblings, land and the warrior spirit. Its day is Tuesday (Mangalvar). Commonly given colours are red and vermilion; the metal is copper; the grain is red lentil (masoor) or toor dal depending on region; the gem is red coral (moonga). Hanuman is the deity most commonly worshipped on Tuesdays in North India, and Kartikeya (Murugan) is also linked with Mangal, especially in the South. These are traditional associations and astrologers hold varying views.

### mangal-practices
- topic: navagraha
- title: Mangal worship practices and beej mantra
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: hanuman
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

On Tuesdays many devotees visit a Hanuman temple, offer red flowers or sindoor, light a lamp and recite the Hanuman Chalisa. Some keep a Tuesday fast (Mangalvar vrat). The beej mantra commonly given for Mangal is "Om Kraam Kreem Kraum Sah Bhaumaya Namah". In Tamil Nadu, Tuesdays are also observed for Murugan and for Amman temples. Practices differ by family and region, and none is required.

### budh-significations
- topic: navagraha
- title: Budh (Mercury) in Vedic astrology
- graha: budh
- weekday: wednesday
- deity: vishnu
- source: Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra
- lang: en

Budh, Mercury, is linked in Jyotish with speech, intellect, learning, trade and communication. Its day is Wednesday (Budhvar). Commonly given colours are green; the metal is bronze or brass in many lists; the grain is green gram (moong); the gem is emerald (panna). Vishnu is the deity commonly connected to Budh, and many families also worship Ganesha on Wednesdays as the deity of beginnings and wisdom. Lists differ between regions and astrologers.

On Wednesdays many devotees worship Ganesha, offering durva grass and modak, and some wear green. Others recite Vishnu Sahasranama or give green moong to those in need. The beej mantra commonly given for Budh is "Om Braam Breem Braum Sah Budhaya Namah". Students sometimes keep Wednesday as a day for prayers before study. Practice varies by region and family.

### guru-significations
- topic: navagraha
- title: Guru or Brihaspati (Jupiter) in Vedic astrology
- graha: guru
- weekday: thursday
- deity: vishnu
- source: Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra
- lang: en

Guru, also called Brihaspati, is Jupiter in Jyotish. He is linked with wisdom, teachers, knowledge, dharma and good fortune, and is regarded as the guru of the devas. Its day is Thursday (Guruvar or Brihaspativar). Commonly given colours are yellow and saffron; the metal is gold; the grain is chana dal (split Bengal gram); the gem is yellow sapphire (pukhraj). Vishnu is the deity most often linked with Thursday, and in some traditions Dattatreya or Brihaspati himself. Associations vary between sources.

### guru-practices
- topic: navagraha
- title: Guru worship practices and beej mantra
- graha: guru
- weekday: thursday
- deity: vishnu
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

On Thursdays many families observe Brihaspati vrat, wear yellow, offer yellow flowers, chana dal and bananas, and worship Vishnu or Lakshmi-Narayan. Some read the Brihaspati Vrat Katha. In many homes people avoid washing hair or cutting nails on this day, a custom that varies by region. The beej mantra commonly given for Guru is "Om Graam Greem Graum Sah Gurave Namah". Respecting teachers is also considered part of Guru worship.

### shukra-significations
- topic: navagraha
- title: Shukra (Venus) in Vedic astrology
- graha: shukra
- weekday: friday
- deity: lakshmi
- source: Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra
- lang: en

Shukra, Venus, is linked in Jyotish with love, beauty, the arts, comfort, marriage and material prosperity. Its day is Friday (Shukravar). Commonly given colours are white, cream and soft pastels, with pink often used in practice; the metal is silver; the grain is rice or white beans in some lists; the gem is diamond (heera) or, as a traditional alternative, white sapphire or zircon. Lakshmi is the deity most commonly worshipped on Fridays, and Santoshi Maa is also worshipped on Fridays in North India. Associations vary.

### shukra-practices
- topic: navagraha
- title: Shukra and Friday worship practices
- graha: shukra
- weekday: friday
- deity: lakshmi
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

On Fridays many devotees worship Lakshmi, light lamps in the evening and keep the home clean, wearing white or light colours. Santoshi Maa vrat is kept on sixteen consecutive Fridays by many devotees, with a custom of avoiding sour foods. Others offer white sweets such as kheer. The beej mantra commonly given for Shukra is "Om Draam Dreem Draum Sah Shukraya Namah". Fasting customs differ by family and region.

### shani-significations
- topic: navagraha
- title: Shani (Saturn) in Vedic astrology
- graha: shani
- weekday: saturday
- deity: shani dev
- source: Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra
- lang: en

Shani, Saturn, is linked in Jyotish with discipline, time, hard work, patience, service and the results of one's actions (karma). Its day is Saturday (Shanivar). Commonly given colours are black and dark blue; the metal is iron; the grains are sesame (til) and black gram (urad); the gem is blue sapphire (neelam), which astrologers usually advise treating with great caution. Shani Dev is the deity of the graha, and Hanuman is also worshipped on Saturdays in many parts of North India. Views differ among astrologers.

### shani-practices
- topic: navagraha
- title: Shani worship practices and beej mantra
- graha: shani
- weekday: saturday
- deity: shani dev
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

On Saturdays devotees commonly visit a Shani temple, offer mustard or sesame oil, black sesame and dark flowers, light a lamp under a peepal tree, or recite the Hanuman Chalisa. Some fast and wear black or dark blue. Charity to the poor, workers and the elderly is also a customary Saturday practice. The beej mantra commonly given is "Om Praam Preem Praum Sah Shanaischaraya Namah". Shani is respected as a teacher of discipline rather than feared, and practice varies by region.

### rahu-significations
- topic: navagraha
- title: Rahu in Vedic astrology
- graha: rahu
- weekday:
- deity: durga
- source: Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra
- lang: en

Rahu is one of the two shadow grahas (chhaya graha) in Jyotish. It is not a physical planet but the Moon's ascending node, treated as a graha. Rahu is linked with ambition, foreign matters, unconventional paths, technology and illusion (maya). It has no weekday of its own; instead a daily period called Rahu Kaal, which falls at a different time each day, is traditionally avoided for starting auspicious work in many regions. Commonly given colours are smoky blue, dark grey or black; the grain is black gram (urad); the gem is hessonite (gomed). Durga is the deity commonly connected with Rahu worship, and Shiva in some traditions.

### rahu-practices
- topic: navagraha
- title: Rahu worship practices and beej mantra
- graha: rahu
- weekday:
- deity: durga
- source: Regional practice (varies)
- lang: en

Customs linked to Rahu include worshipping Durga, especially on Tuesdays and Fridays, offering lamps at Rahu Kaal in some South Indian temples, and giving black gram or blue cloth in charity. The beej mantra commonly given is "Om Bhraam Bhreem Bhraum Sah Rahave Namah". The legend behind Rahu comes from the Puranic story of the churning of the ocean, where a demon drank the nectar of immortality and was severed by Vishnu. Practices differ widely and none is considered compulsory.

### ketu-significations
- topic: navagraha
- title: Ketu in Vedic astrology
- graha: ketu
- weekday:
- deity: ganesha
- source: Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra
- lang: en

Ketu is the second shadow graha, the Moon's descending node. In Jyotish it is linked with detachment, spiritual insight, moksha (liberation) and past-life tendencies, and is described as the headless counterpart of Rahu. It has no weekday of its own. Commonly given colours are grey, brown or multicoloured; the grain is horse gram (kulthi); the gem is cat's eye (lehsunia). Ganesha is the deity most often linked with Ketu worship, and in some traditions Bhairava or Shiva. Associations vary between lineages.

### ketu-practices
- topic: navagraha
- title: Ketu worship practices and beej mantra
- graha: ketu
- weekday:
- deity: ganesha
- source: Regional practice (varies)
- lang: en

Customs linked to Ketu include worshipping Ganesha, feeding dogs, visiting temples with a spiritual focus, and giving blankets or sesame in charity. The beej mantra commonly given is "Om Sraam Sreem Sraum Sah Ketave Namah". In the Puranic story, Rahu and Ketu are the two halves of the same demon; Ketu is the body and Rahu is the head. Practices differ between regions and families, and none is considered required.

### navagraha-temple-parikrama
- topic: navagraha
- title: Navagraha shrine and circumambulation custom
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Many temples, especially in South India, have a Navagraha shrine where the nine grahas stand on a platform, with Surya in the centre and the others around him. Devotees often walk around it (parikrama) an odd number of times, commonly nine, reciting the beej mantras or offering lamps. Some Tamil Nadu pilgrims visit a circuit of nine temples near Kumbakonam, each linked to one graha. Customs differ by temple, and each temple's priest is the right guide for local practice.

### navagraha-gemstones-note
- topic: navagraha
- title: Gemstones and the grahas, a traditional note
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Jyotish traditionally links each graha with a gem: ruby for Surya, pearl for Chandra, red coral for Mangal, emerald for Budh, yellow sapphire for Guru, diamond for Shukra, blue sapphire for Shani, hessonite for Rahu, and cat's eye for Ketu. Astrologers generally advise consulting a knowledgeable person before wearing any gem, since opinions on suitability differ. Gems are a matter of personal belief and are not a substitute for professional advice, so Pandit ji may mention the tradition but should not recommend buying a stone.
