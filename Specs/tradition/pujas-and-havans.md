# Pujas and havans

### puja-what-is-a-puja
- topic: puja
- title: What a puja is, and the sankalp
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

A puja is a ritual of worship in which a deity is honoured with offerings such as flowers, lamps, incense, water, fruit and sweets. A full puja is traditionally described as having sixteen offerings (shodashopachara), though simpler forms are common. It usually begins with a sankalp, a spoken statement of intent that names the worshipper, family lineage (gotra), place and date, and the purpose of the prayer. Pujas are devotional acts, and families decide for themselves how simple or elaborate they wish them to be.

### puja-what-is-a-havan
- topic: puja
- title: What a havan or homa is
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

A havan, also called a homa or yajna, is a fire ritual in which ghee, grains, herbs and wood are offered into a consecrated fire (Agni) while mantras are chanted, with the word "swaha" at each offering. In tradition, Agni carries the offerings to the deities. Havans are performed at weddings, housewarmings, festivals and for specific deities such as Navagraha or Durga. Different woods and materials are prescribed for different purposes, and a qualified priest usually conducts them.

### puja-muhurat-note
- topic: puja
- title: Muhurat, the auspicious time for a puja
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

A muhurat is a time considered auspicious for beginning a ceremony, worked out by a priest from the panchang (Hindu almanac), which uses the lunar day (tithi), weekday, star (nakshatra) and other factors. Weddings, Griha Pravesh and major pujas are usually fixed by muhurat. Panchang calculations vary slightly between regions, so a local pandit's date is the one to follow. A simple puja at home can be done any day, and many families treat the muhurat as helpful rather than essential.

### puja-rudrabhishek
- topic: puja
- title: Rudrabhishek
- graha: chandra
- weekday: monday
- deity: shiva
- source: Shiva Purana
- lang: en

Rudrabhishek is a ritual bathing (abhishek) of the Shivling, in which water, milk, curd, honey, ghee, sugar and other substances are poured while the Sri Rudram hymn from the Yajurveda is chanted. The Shiva Purana praises the abhishek of Shiva. It is traditionally performed on Mondays, Pradosh, Maha Shivaratri and in the month of Shravan, and by families at temples such as Mahakaleshwar or Kashi Vishwanath. Devotees offer it as an act of devotion, and the format varies by region and temple.

### puja-satyanarayan-katha
- topic: puja
- title: Satyanarayan Puja and Katha
- graha: guru
- weekday: thursday
- deity: vishnu
- source: Skanda Purana
- lang: en

The Satyanarayan Puja honours Vishnu as the lord of truth (Satya-Narayan). It includes a reading of a katha, a story in five chapters, traditionally attributed to the Skanda Purana, about devotees who kept faith in the vrat. It is performed on Purnima, on Thursdays, and at occasions such as a new home, a wedding or a new business, mainly in North, Western and Eastern India. The prasad is a sweet made from semolina, ghee, sugar and banana, often with panchamrit. Practice varies between regions.

### puja-navagraha-shanti
- topic: puja
- title: Navagraha Shanti Puja and Homa
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

The Navagraha Shanti puja worships all nine grahas, usually with a homa in which each graha receives its own mantra and its traditional wood: arka for Surya, palasha for Chandra, khadira for Mangal, apamarga for Budh, peepal for Guru, udumbara (fig) for Shukra, shami for Shani, durva grass for Rahu and kusha grass for Ketu. Families may arrange it before a new venture, at Griha Pravesh, on a birthday or when an astrologer suggests it. Shanti means "peace", and the ritual is a prayer for harmony rather than a promise of any outcome.

### puja-shani-shanti-tailabhishek
- topic: puja
- title: Shani Shanti and Tailabhishek
- graha: shani
- weekday: saturday
- deity: shani dev
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Shani Shanti puja is a prayer to Shani Dev, often done on Saturdays or Shani Amavasya. In Tailabhishek, sesame or mustard oil is poured on the Shani idol or stone while mantras are chanted, with black sesame, dark flowers and blue cloth as offerings. The Shani Stotra and Hanuman Chalisa may be recited. Many devotees perform it during Sade Sati or when an astrologer suggests it, as a customary way of showing respect to Shani. Temples such as Shani Shingnapur and Thirunallar are well-known centres.

### puja-mangal-shanti
- topic: puja
- title: Mangal Shanti or Mangal Dosha puja
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: hanuman
- source: Regional practice (varies)
- lang: en

The Mangal Shanti puja is a prayer to Mangal (Mars), often done on Tuesdays, sometimes at the Mangalnath temple in Ujjain, which tradition regards as the birthplace of Mangal. It may include a homa, red lentils, red cloth and the recitation of Mangal mantras or the Hanuman Chalisa. Families sometimes arrange it when a horoscope is said to show Mangal Dosha. In some regions a symbolic ritual called Kumbh Vivah is also done before a wedding. Astrologers differ in how much weight they give these customs.

### puja-kaal-sarp-dosha
- topic: puja
- title: Kaal Sarp Dosh puja
- graha: rahu
- weekday:
- deity: shiva
- source: Regional practice (varies)
- lang: en

Kaal Sarp Dosh puja is a prayer performed when an astrologer notes that a horoscope has all planets between Rahu and Ketu. It is commonly done at Trimbakeshwar near Nashik, and at places like Ujjain, Srikalahasti and Prayagraj, with worship of Shiva and of the serpent deities (Nag). Devotees often do it on Nag Panchami or on certain new moon days. The idea of Kaal Sarp Dosh is not found in the older classical texts such as Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra, and astrologers disagree about it.

### puja-pitra-shraddha
- topic: puja
- title: Pitra Tarpan and Shraddha, honouring ancestors
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Shraddha is the Hindu rite of remembering and honouring one's ancestors (pitras) with food, water offerings (tarpan) and prayers, performed on the death anniversary and during Pitru Paksha, a fortnight in the lunar month of Bhadrapada or Ashwin. The final day, Sarva Pitru Amavasya, is used for all ancestors. Pind daan is done at places like Gaya, Varanasi and Haridwar. Families feed Brahmins, cows, crows and dogs, and give to the needy. At its heart the rite is gratitude and remembrance, and customs vary by community.

### puja-maha-mrityunjaya-jaap
- topic: puja
- title: Maha Mrityunjaya Jaap
- graha:
- weekday: monday
- deity: shiva
- source: Shiva Purana
- lang: en

The Maha Mrityunjaya Mantra ("Om Tryambakam Yajamahe ...") is a Vedic prayer to Shiva that appears in the Rigveda and is widely used in the Shiva tradition. A jaap, repeated chanting, is commonly done 108 times a day, or in larger counts by priests over several days. Devotees recite it with prayers for the health and wellbeing of a named person or family. It is a prayer and a devotional practice, and not a substitute for medical care. It is often chanted on Mondays or during Shravan.

### puja-lakshmi-puja
- topic: puja
- title: Lakshmi Puja
- graha: shukra
- weekday: friday
- deity: lakshmi
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Lakshmi Puja is performed at home on Fridays by many families, and on the new moon night of Diwali, when Lakshmi is worshipped along with Ganesha, often at the Pradosh time in the evening. Homes are cleaned, lit with diyas and decorated with rangoli. Offerings include lotus or red flowers, sweets, coins and the Sri Suktam or Lakshmi Ashtottara may be recited. Business families perform Chopda Pujan, worshipping new account books. Customs vary by region.

### puja-ganesh-puja
- topic: puja
- title: Ganesh Puja
- graha:
- weekday: wednesday
- deity: ganesha
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Ganesh Puja is the worship of Ganesha, and in Hindu custom he is invoked first at the start of any ceremony, wedding or new venture. It is performed on Wednesdays, Chaturthi days and above all at Ganesh Chaturthi, when clay idols are installed for a number of days and later immersed. Offerings include modak, durva grass and red hibiscus, and the Ganapati Atharvashirsha may be recited. In Maharashtra and many parts of India it is also a community festival.

### puja-sundarkand-path
- topic: puja
- title: Sundarkand Path
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: hanuman
- source: Valmiki Ramayana
- lang: en

Sundarkand is the fifth book of the Valmiki Ramayana and also of Tulsidas's Ramcharitmanas, telling of Hanuman's journey to Lanka and his meeting with Sita. A Sundarkand Path is a recitation of this book, commonly in the Tulsidas version in North India, often over one sitting and with a kalash and lamp. Devotees do it on Tuesdays and Saturdays, at a new home, or at family gatherings. It is a devotional reading, and groups sometimes sing it together with music.

### puja-hanuman-chalisa-path
- topic: puja
- title: Hanuman Chalisa Path
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: hanuman
- source: Hanuman Chalisa by Tulsidas
- lang: en

The Hanuman Chalisa is a forty-verse hymn to Hanuman written by Tulsidas in Awadhi. Many devotees recite it daily, and on Tuesdays and Saturdays in particular; some read it 11 or 108 times at special occasions such as Hanuman Jayanti or before a new venture. It can be done at home, in a temple or in a group (satsang), with a lamp lit before an image of Hanuman. It is a devotional practice, and families differ in how many repetitions they choose.

### puja-durga-saptashati-path
- topic: puja
- title: Durga Saptashati Path
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity: durga
- source: Devi Mahatmya (Durga Saptashati)
- lang: en

The Durga Saptashati, also called the Devi Mahatmya or Chandi Path, has about seven hundred verses in thirteen chapters and is part of the Markandeya Purana. It is read especially during Navratri, either in full or a few chapters each day, often preceded by the Kavach, Argala and Keelak stotras. Many families have it recited by a priest and conclude with a havan on Ashtami or Navami. It is a devotional text of the Goddess, and the format differs between Shakta traditions.

### puja-griha-pravesh
- topic: puja
- title: Griha Pravesh, the housewarming ceremony
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity: ganesha
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Griha Pravesh is the ceremony for first entering a new home. A priest sets an auspicious date, and the family usually enters with a kalash, a lamp and Ganesha worship, followed by a Vastu or Navagraha puja and often a Satyanarayan Katha. In many North Indian families milk is boiled over a stove until it overflows, as a sign of abundance, and the doorway is decorated with a mango-leaf toran and rangoli. Customs differ by region, and the date is chosen from the panchang.
