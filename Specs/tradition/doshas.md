# Doshas

### dosha-general-approach
- topic: dosha
- title: How doshas are described in Jyotish
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

In Jyotish a dosha is a planetary configuration that astrologers describe as carrying challenges, and tradition also lists remedies (upaya) such as prayer, charity and ritual. Astrologers differ widely on which doshas exist, how a chart is read, and how much weight to give them, and many stress that a full chart has other supportive factors. Pandit ji should describe doshas as what tradition says, invite the person to consult a qualified astrologer, and avoid fear language or any claim that a remedy changes an outcome.

### dosha-mangal-what
- topic: dosha
- title: Mangal Dosha (Manglik), what tradition says
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: hanuman
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Mangal Dosha, also called Kuja Dosha or being "Manglik", is said in Jyotish to arise when Mars is placed in certain houses of the birth chart, most often the first, fourth, seventh, eighth or twelfth, and in some schools the second. It is mainly discussed in connection with marriage compatibility. Tradition also lists cancelling conditions, for example when both partners are Manglik or when Mars is in its own or exalted sign. Because many charts are said to qualify, astrologers differ about how much weight it deserves.

### dosha-mangal-remedies
- topic: dosha
- title: Mangal Dosha, traditionally prescribed practices
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: hanuman
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Practices traditionally linked with Mangal Dosha include Tuesday worship of Hanuman, reciting the Hanuman Chalisa, a Mangal Shanti puja (often at the Mangalnath temple in Ujjain), keeping a Tuesday fast, offering red lentils and jaggery in charity, and chanting the Mangal beej mantra. In some regions a symbolic Kumbh Vivah is done. Families choose among these according to their tradition, and astrologers differ on what is advisable. These are customs, not treatments, and no outcome is promised.

### dosha-kaalsarp-what
- topic: dosha
- title: Kaal Sarp Dosh, what tradition says
- graha: rahu
- weekday:
- deity: shiva
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Kaal Sarp Dosh is said to occur when all seven classical planets lie on one side of the Rahu-Ketu axis in a birth chart. Modern astrologers list twelve types, named for the house of Rahu, such as Anant, Kulik, Vasuki, Shankhpal, Padma, Mahapadma, Takshak, Karkotak, Shankhchud, Ghatak, Vishdhar and Sheshnag. The concept is not found in Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra and is considered by some astrologers a later, modern addition, while others follow it closely. Opinions differ, and it should be described as a debated tradition.

### dosha-kaalsarp-remedies
- topic: dosha
- title: Kaal Sarp Dosh, traditionally prescribed practices
- graha: rahu
- weekday:
- deity: shiva
- source: Regional practice (varies)
- lang: en

Practices linked with Kaal Sarp Dosh include a Kaal Sarp puja at places like Trimbakeshwar, Ujjain or Srikalahasti, worship of Shiva with Rudrabhishek, honouring Nag (serpent) deities on Nag Panchami, chanting the Maha Mrityunjaya Mantra and giving a pair of silver serpents in ritual. Some devotees also visit the Shiva temple on Mondays. These are traditions followed by some families, and no result is promised.

### dosha-sadesati-what
- topic: dosha
- title: Sade Sati, what it means in Jyotish
- graha: shani
- weekday: saturday
- deity: shani dev
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Sade Sati ("seven and a half") is the period of about seven and a half years when Saturn passes through three zodiac signs: the one before the Moon's sign in a person's birth chart, the Moon's own sign, and the one after it. Each sign takes about two and a half years, and it happens roughly every thirty years since Saturn takes about 29 years to circle the zodiac. Many astrologers describe it as a period of testing, discipline and slow progress, and effects are said to depend on the whole chart.

### dosha-dhaiya
- topic: dosha
- title: Shani Dhaiya (Small Panoti)
- graha: shani
- weekday: saturday
- deity: shani dev
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Shani Dhaiya, also called Small Panoti or Ashtama and Kantak Shani, is the period of about two and a half years when Saturn passes through the fourth or eighth sign from the Moon's birth sign. Astrologers describe it as a period for patience and care in decisions, and views on its effects vary a great deal between schools. As with Sade Sati, the result is said to depend on the full chart. Many people observe it by prayers to Shani Dev, which is a personal choice.

### dosha-sadesati-practices
- topic: dosha
- title: Sade Sati, traditionally observed practices
- graha: shani
- weekday: saturday
- deity: shani dev
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

During Sade Sati, devotees traditionally worship Shani Dev and Hanuman on Saturdays, light a sesame or mustard oil lamp, reciting the Shani Chalisa or Hanuman Chalisa, give sesame, black cloth or oil to those in need, and serve the elderly and workers. Some visit Shani temples such as Shingnapur. The tradition presents Shani as a fair teacher of karma, so honest work and kindness are considered part of the practice. These are customs, not promised remedies.

### dosha-pitra-what
- topic: dosha
- title: Pitra Dosha, what tradition says
- graha: surya
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Pitra Dosha is a term used by some astrologers for an afflicted placement of the Sun or the ninth house of a birth chart, which they link with the family's duties to its ancestors (pitras). Tradition teaches that remembering ancestors with shraddha and tarpan is a duty of gratitude. The concept is a later development and astrologers differ on it. It is best presented as an invitation to honour one's ancestors and not as a cause for worry.

### dosha-pitra-practices
- topic: dosha
- title: Pitra Dosha, traditionally observed practices
- graha: surya
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Practices linked with honouring ancestors include shraddha and tarpan during Pitru Paksha, especially on Sarva Pitru Amavasya, pind daan at Gaya, feeding Brahmins, cows, crows and dogs, giving to the needy, planting a peepal tree and offering water to the Sun. Some families also read the Bhagavad Gita during this period. These are acts of remembrance, and families follow them as their custom and means allow.
