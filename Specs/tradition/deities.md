# Deities

### shiva-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Shiva, iconography and symbols
- graha:
- weekday: monday
- deity: shiva
- source: Shiva Purana
- lang: en

Shiva is worshipped as the great ascetic and the lord of Kailash. Common iconography shows a third eye on the forehead, a crescent moon and the river Ganga in his matted locks, a serpent around his neck, ash on his body, a trishul (trident) and a damru (small drum). His vahana (vehicle) is Nandi the bull. He is worshipped most often as the aniconic Shivling. The Shiva Purana is the text most associated with his worship, and Shiva is also revered in the Vedic Rudram hymn. Shaiva traditions differ between regions.

Shiva is traditionally worshipped on Mondays, on Pradosh (the twilight of the thirteenth lunar day), and during the month of Shravan. Maha Shivaratri is his major festival. Devotees offer water, milk, bael leaves and white flowers, and often wear white or ash-grey. Well-known mantras are "Om Namah Shivaya", the Maha Mrityunjaya Mantra and the Shiva Panchakshara Stotra. Some South Indian traditions also observe Monday, and Shaiva communities differ in their rituals.

### parvati-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Parvati (Gauri), iconography and symbols
- graha:
- weekday:

- source: Shiva Purana
- lang: en

Parvati, the daughter of the mountain king Himavan, is the consort of Shiva and the mother of Ganesha and Kartikeya. She is shown as a gentle, graceful goddess, often seated beside Shiva, sometimes holding a lotus, and is also called Gauri, Uma and Shakti. She is regarded in Shakta tradition as an aspect of the Mother Goddess, and her fierce forms include Durga. The Shiva Purana tells of her penance to win Shiva as her husband.

Parvati as Gauri is worshipped in festivals such as Hartalika Teej, Gangaur in Rajasthan and Gauri Puja, which are particularly observed by women for marital happiness in a devotional sense. Red, green and yellow are commonly worn at these festivals, with green especially popular in Teej. Gauri is also honoured on Mondays together with Shiva, and during Navratri as part of the Goddess. A commonly used mantra is "Om Gauryai Namah". Customs differ widely between regions and families.

### ganesha-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Ganesha, iconography and symbols
- graha:
- weekday: wednesday
- deity: ganesha
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Ganesha, the son of Shiva and Parvati, is shown with an elephant head, a large belly, one tusk broken or whole, and usually four arms. He commonly holds a pasha (noose), an ankusha (goad), a modak sweet and a broken tusk or lotus. His vahana is the mooshak, a mouse. The elephant head is read by devotees as a symbol of wisdom and a large capacity to listen; the mouse as the mastery of small desires. He is Vighnaharta, the remover of obstacles, and is invoked first in most Hindu rites.

### ganesha-worship
- topic: deity
- title: Ganesha, days, colours, festivals and mantras
- graha: ketu
- weekday: wednesday
- deity: ganesha
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Ganesha is traditionally worshipped on Wednesdays, and on Chaturthi (the fourth lunar day) each month; Sankashti Chaturthi after the full moon is widely kept, and in Maharashtra the Tuesday Sankashti is called Angarki. Ganesh Chaturthi is his major festival. Red, orange, yellow and green are commonly used; offerings include modak, durva grass and red hibiscus. Well-known mantras are "Om Gan Ganapataye Namah" and "Vakratunda Mahakaya". The Ganapati Atharvashirsha is the text associated with his worship. Practice varies by region.

### vishnu-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Vishnu, iconography and symbols
- graha:
- weekday: thursday
- deity: vishnu
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Vishnu, the preserver in the Hindu trinity, is shown with blue skin, four arms and a crown, holding a shankh (conch), the Sudarshan Chakra (discus), a gada (mace) and a padma (lotus). He reclines on the serpent Sheshnag in the cosmic ocean, with Lakshmi at his feet, or rides Garuda the eagle. Tradition describes ten principal avatars (Dashavatara), including Rama and Krishna. Vaishnava traditions across India worship him under many names, such as Narayana, Hari and Venkateswara.

Vishnu is traditionally worshipped on Thursdays and on Ekadashi, the eleventh lunar day, which many Vaishnavas observe with a fast. Yellow, the colour of his pitambar (yellow cloth), and saffron are commonly used, with tulsi leaves as a key offering. Well-known mantras are "Om Namo Narayanaya" and "Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya", and the Vishnu Sahasranama is widely recited. Major observances include Vaikuntha Ekadashi and Devshayani Ekadashi. Practice varies among Vaishnava communities.

### krishna-iconography

- title: Krishna, iconography and symbols

Krishna, an avatar of Vishnu, is shown with dark or blue skin, a peacock feather in his crown, a flute (bansuri), a yellow dhoti and often with cows or as a butter-loving child (Bal Gopal). He appears in the Mahabharata and Bhagavata Purana, and the Bhagavad Gita records his teaching to Arjuna. Radha is commonly shown with him in devotional traditions. The flute is read by devotees as a symbol of the soul's call to the divine.

Krishna is celebrated at Janmashtami, his birth festival, and at Holi, Govardhan Puja and Radhashtami in many regions. Yellow and blue are commonly worn, as is peacock-feather imagery. Many devotees also keep Ekadashi. Well-known mantras include the Hare Krishna Maha Mantra and "Om Namo Bhagavate Vasudevaya". Krishna bhakti has many streams, such as Vallabha, Gaudiya and Pushtimarg, with differing customs. He has no single fixed weekday.

### rama-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Rama, iconography and symbols
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity: rama
- source: Valmiki Ramayana
- lang: en

Rama, an avatar of Vishnu and the hero of the Ramayana of Valmiki, is shown with blue or dark skin, a bow (kodanda) and arrows, usually with Sita and Lakshmana, and with Hanuman kneeling at his feet. He is called Maryada Purushottam, the ideal of dharma and duty. The Ramcharitmanas of Tulsidas is the best-known retelling in much of North India. Rama's capital is traditionally Ayodhya. His dynasty is the Suryavansha, the solar line.

### rama-worship
- topic: deity
- title: Rama, festivals, colours and mantras
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity: rama
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Rama is celebrated at Ram Navami, and the story of his return to Ayodhya is marked at Diwali and Dussehra, with Ramlila performances in many towns. Saffron, yellow and blue are common colours. A well-loved chant is "Shri Ram Jai Ram Jai Jai Ram", and "Jai Shri Ram" is a common greeting; the Ram Raksha Stotra is also widely recited. Rama has no single fixed weekday, and many families also honour him on Thursdays or Tuesdays alongside Hanuman, depending on custom.

### hanuman-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Hanuman, iconography and symbols
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: hanuman
- source: Hanuman Chalisa by Tulsidas
- lang: en

Hanuman, the devoted servant of Rama, is shown as a powerful monkey-faced figure, often red or vermilion in colour, carrying a gada (mace) and sometimes the Sanjeevani mountain. A popular image shows him tearing open his chest to reveal Rama and Sita in his heart. The Hanuman Chalisa, a forty-verse hymn by Tulsidas, praises his strength, devotion and humility. He is regarded as a son of Vayu, the wind god, and as a Chiranjivi, one who lives forever.

### hanuman-worship
- topic: deity
- title: Hanuman, days, colours, festivals and mantras
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: hanuman
- source: Common North Indian practice

Hanuman is traditionally worshipped on Tuesdays and Saturdays. His main festival is Hanuman Jayanti, observed on Chaitra Purnima in most of North India, while some regions, such as Tamil Nadu and Kerala, observe it at different dates. Red, orange and saffron are commonly used, with sindoor and jasmine oil offered at many temples. Common mantras include "Om Hanumate Namah" and "Jai Shri Ram". Reciting the Hanuman Chalisa is the most widespread practice.

### lakshmi-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Lakshmi, iconography and symbols
- graha: shukra
- weekday: friday
- deity: lakshmi
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Lakshmi, the consort of Vishnu, is the goddess of wealth, prosperity and good fortune. She is shown seated or standing on a lotus, with gold coins pouring from one hand, and often flanked by two elephants (Gaja Lakshmi). Her vahana is the owl in many depictions. Tradition names eight forms called Ashta Lakshmi. The Sri Suktam is the Vedic hymn most associated with her. Her symbols are read by devotees as prosperity, purity and generosity, not only as material wealth.

### lakshmi-worship
- topic: deity
- title: Lakshmi, days, colours, festivals and mantras
- graha: shukra
- weekday: friday
- deity: lakshmi
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Lakshmi is traditionally worshipped on Fridays, and especially on Diwali night (Lakshmi Puja). Other observances include Sharad Purnima, Varalakshmi Vratam in South India and Dhanteras. Red, pink, gold and white are commonly used, with red and gold favoured on Diwali. Well-known mantras are "Om Shreem Mahalakshmyai Namah" and the Mahalakshmi Ashtakam. Many homes light lamps and keep the entrance clean for her. Customs vary between regions.

### saraswati-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Saraswati, iconography and symbols
- graha: budh
- weekday:
- deity: saraswati
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Saraswati is the goddess of knowledge, music, speech and the arts. She is shown in white, seated on a white lotus or riding a hamsa (swan), holding a veena (stringed instrument), a book and a mala (rosary). The swan is read by devotees as the ability to discern truth, as the legend says it separates milk from water. Saraswati is also a sacred river in the Rigveda. Students and artists often keep her image near their books and instruments.

Saraswati is celebrated at Vasant Panchami, in January or February, with yellow clothes and offerings, and during Navratri, especially in the South and in Bengal as Saraswati Puja on the last days. Many families perform Vidyarambham, a child's first writing, in her name. White and yellow are commonly used. A well-known mantra is "Om Aim Saraswatyai Namah". Some families link Wednesdays or Thursdays with her, but there is no single fixed day.

### durga-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Durga, iconography and symbols
- graha:
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: durga
- source: Devi Mahatmya (Durga Saptashati)
- lang: en

Durga is the warrior form of the Divine Mother. She is shown with ten arms holding weapons given by the gods, such as a trident, a discus, a sword and a bow, riding a lion or tiger. The Devi Mahatmya, also called the Durga Saptashati and part of the Markandeya Purana, narrates her victory over the buffalo demon Mahishasura. Devotees read the image as the triumph of good over evil and as protection of dharma. Regional forms include Vaishno Devi and Ambaji.

Durga is traditionally worshipped on Tuesdays and Fridays, on Ashtami, and during Navratri and Durga Puja. Red is the most common colour, with orange and yellow also popular. Well-known mantras are "Om Dum Durgayai Namah" and the Navarna mantra "Om Aim Hreem Kleem Chamundayai Vichche". Reciting the Durga Saptashati, Durga Chalisa or Durga Kavach is customary. Bengal, Gujarat and other regions each have their own style of celebration.

### kali-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Kali, iconography and symbols
- graha: rahu
- weekday:
- deity: kali
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Kali is a form of the Mother Goddess, shown with dark skin, a garland of skulls or heads, a sword, a severed head and a lolling tongue, standing on Shiva. She is worshipped in Shakta tradition, especially in Bengal, Assam and Odisha, and at Kalighat in Kolkata. Devotional poets such as Ramprasad Sen sang to her as a loving mother. Devotees read her fierce image as the power of time and the destruction of ego and illusion, not as a figure of ill will. Interpretations differ between schools.

Kali is traditionally worshipped on Amavasya (new moon) nights, and Kali Puja is celebrated in Bengal on the new moon of the Diwali period. She is also worshipped during Navratri and by some on Tuesdays and Fridays. Black, red and deep blue are commonly used, with red hibiscus as a traditional offering. A well-known mantra is "Om Kreem Kalikayai Namah". Her worship has distinct regional and lineage rules, so a family's own tradition or priest is the best guide.

### surya-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Surya Dev, iconography and symbols
- graha: surya
- weekday: sunday
- deity: surya
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Surya, the Sun god, is shown standing or riding a chariot drawn by seven horses, driven by the charioteer Aruna, and holding lotus flowers in his hands. The seven horses are often read as the seven days of the week or the seven colours of light. The famous Sun Temple at Konark in Odisha is built as a giant chariot. Surya is honoured as the visible deity, whose daily rising can be seen by all, and the Gayatri Mantra is addressed to him as Savitr.

### surya-worship
- topic: deity
- title: Surya, days, colours, festivals and mantras
- graha: surya
- weekday: sunday
- deity: surya
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Surya is traditionally worshipped on Sundays, at sunrise. Observances include Makar Sankranti, Ratha Saptami and Chhath Puja, in which devotees offer arghya to the setting and rising Sun. Red, orange and saffron are commonly used, with red flowers and water offered in a copper vessel. Well-known mantras include "Om Suryaya Namah" and the Aditya Hridayam. Sun worship is also central to Surya Namaskar practice. Customs differ by region, with Bihar and Jharkhand especially known for Chhath.

### shani-dev-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Shani Dev, iconography and symbols
- graha: shani
- weekday: saturday
- deity: shani dev
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Shani Dev is the deity of the graha Saturn. He is shown with dark skin, wearing black or dark blue, carrying a bow or a trident, and riding a crow or vulture. Puranic tradition describes him as a son of Surya and Chhaya, and as a god of justice who is believed to give results according to one's karma. Well-known temples include Shani Shingnapur in Maharashtra and Thirunallar in Tamil Nadu. Devotees are encouraged to see him as a stern teacher rather than a figure to fear.

Shani Dev is traditionally worshipped on Saturdays, on Shani Amavasya (a Saturday new moon) and on Shani Jayanti, his birth festival on the new moon of the Jyeshtha month in many calendars. Black and dark blue are commonly used, with mustard oil, sesame and iron items as offerings. A well-known mantra is "Om Sham Shanaishcharaya Namah". Many also recite the Hanuman Chalisa on Saturdays. Customs vary by region and family.

### kartikeya-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Kartikeya (Murugan), iconography and symbols
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: kartikeya
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Kartikeya, also called Skanda, Murugan, Subramanya and Kumara, is the son of Shiva and Parvati and the commander of the divine army. He is shown as a youthful warrior with a vel (spear), riding a peacock, and in some forms with six faces (Shanmukha). He is especially beloved in Tamil Nadu, where he is the chief deity of the Tamil people, and also worshipped in Karnataka, Kerala and Maharashtra. The peacock is read as the taming of pride.

- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: kartikeya

Kartikeya is traditionally worshipped on Tuesdays, on Shashti (the sixth lunar day) and at festivals such as Skanda Shashti, Thaipusam and Panguni Uthiram, mainly in the South and among the Tamil diaspora. Red, orange and yellow are commonly used. Well-known chants include "Om Saravana Bhava" and "Om Shanmukhaya Namah". Pilgrimage to the six major Murugan temples (Arupadai Veedu) is a notable devotional tradition. Practice varies by region and community.

### santoshi-maa-iconography
- topic: deity
- title: Santoshi Maa, iconography and symbols
- graha: shukra
- weekday: friday
- deity: santoshi maa
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Santoshi Maa is a goddess of contentment and satisfaction, shown as a gentle goddess seated on a lotus, holding a sword or trident and a kalash or bowl. In popular tradition she is a daughter of Ganesha. Her devotion is not described in the older Puranas and became widespread in North India in the second half of the twentieth century, helped by the 1975 film "Jai Santoshi Maa". She is mainly worshipped by women in North and Central India.

### santoshi-maa-worship
- topic: deity
- title: Santoshi Maa, vrat, colours and offerings
- graha: shukra
- weekday: friday
- deity: santoshi maa
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Santoshi Maa is traditionally worshipped on Fridays. Her best-known vrat is kept for sixteen consecutive Fridays, ending with a udyapan (concluding ceremony). The offering is gur (jaggery) and roasted chana (gram), and many observers avoid sour foods during the fast. Saffron, yellow and red are commonly seen in her imagery. The Santoshi Maa Vrat Katha is read, and a simple mantra is "Om Shri Santoshi Matre Namah". Customs vary by family and region.
