# Weekdays and vrats

### weekday-overview-vara
- topic: weekday
- title: How the seven weekdays link to grahas and deities
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

In Hindu tradition each weekday (vara) is linked to a graha, and by custom to one or more deities: Sunday to Surya, Monday to Chandra and Shiva, Tuesday to Mangal and Hanuman, Wednesday to Budh and Vishnu or Ganesha, Thursday to Guru and Vishnu, Friday to Shukra and Lakshmi or Devi, Saturday to Shani and Hanuman. The pairings of deity with day are popular custom and differ between regions and families. A vrat is a vow or fast kept on a chosen day; it is optional and personal.

### weekday-design-approach
- topic: weekday
- title: Why a design is suggested for a weekday
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Modern convention
- lang: en

When Pandit ji suggests a T-shirt design for a weekday, it is a style idea built on the traditional day-deity pairings, for example a Hanuman design for Tuesday or a Lakshmi design for Friday. It is not a ritual requirement, and wearing a design does not replace worship or carry any promised result. Colours suggested follow commonly given customs, which differ by region. Devotees may simply enjoy wearing the symbols and colours of the deity they feel close to, on any day.

### sunday-overview
- topic: weekday
- title: Sunday (Ravivar): Surya's day
- graha: surya
- weekday: sunday
- deity: surya
- source: Brihat Parashara Hora Shastra
- lang: en

Sunday (Ravivar or Adityavar) is the day of Surya, the Sun. Commonly linked colours are red, orange and saffron. Surya is worshipped by offering water at sunrise and reciting Surya mantras or the Aditya Hridayam. In some regions Sunday is also associated with Surya-linked forms such as Suryanarayana, and with Bhairava in a few traditions. The graha-day link comes from Jyotish, while the deity pairings vary by community.

### sunday-vrat
- topic: weekday
- title: Sunday vrat customs
- graha: surya
- weekday: sunday
- deity: surya
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Devotees who keep a Sunday vrat for Surya often rise before sunrise, bathe, offer water to the Sun and eat only one meal, commonly without salt, after sunset or in the afternoon. Many read the Ravivar Vrat Katha. Wearing red or orange is customary. Some tie the fast to a set number of Sundays, such as twelve. Customs vary by family, and anyone with health needs should adapt a fast sensibly.

### sunday-design-idea
- topic: weekday
- title: Design ideas for Sunday
- graha: surya
- weekday: sunday
- deity: surya
- source: Modern convention
- lang: en

For Sunday, a design inspired by Surya fits the tradition: a radiant sun, a sun mandala, the seven-horse chariot of Surya, or an Om with sun rays. Saffron, orange, red or copper tones follow the commonly given colours for the day. The seven horses are often read as the seven days or the seven colours of light. These are style suggestions based on custom, not a ritual requirement.

### monday-overview
- topic: weekday
- title: Monday (Somvar): Shiva and Chandra's day
- graha: chandra
- weekday: monday
- deity: shiva
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Monday (Somvar) is the day of Chandra, the Moon, and is widely observed as Shiva's day, since Shiva wears the crescent moon on his head and is called Somanath or Chandrashekhar. Commonly worn colours are white and cream. Devotees offer water, milk, bael leaves (bilva patra) and white flowers to the Shivling. The Shravan month Mondays are especially observed. Observance is optional and varies by region.

### monday-vrat
- topic: weekday
- title: Somvar vrat customs
- graha: chandra
- weekday: monday
- deity: shiva
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Many devotees keep a Monday fast (Somvar vrat) for Shiva, taking one meal or fruits and milk, visiting a Shiva temple and reciting "Om Namah Shivaya". Some keep sixteen Mondays (Solah Somvar), a custom popular in parts of North and Western India, and the Somvar Vrat Katha is read. Many wear white. Customs differ by family; the fast is a personal choice and does not require elaborate arrangements.

### monday-design-idea
- topic: weekday
- title: Design ideas for Monday
- graha: chandra
- weekday: monday
- deity: shiva
- source: Modern convention
- lang: en

For Monday, designs linked to Shiva follow the tradition: the trishul, the damru, a crescent moon, the Shivling, Nandi the bull, or "Om Namah Shivaya" in Devanagari. White, cream, light grey or soft blue tones follow the commonly given colours for the day. Shiva imagery is popular year-round and is not limited to Mondays. These are style suggestions, not a ritual requirement.

### tuesday-overview
- topic: weekday
- title: Tuesday (Mangalvar): Hanuman and Mangal's day
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: hanuman
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Tuesday (Mangalvar) is the day of Mangal, Mars. In North India it is widely observed as Hanuman's day, and in South India Tuesdays are also kept for Kartikeya (Murugan) and for the Goddess. Commonly worn colours are red, vermilion and orange. Devotees visit Hanuman temples, offer sindoor, red flowers and boondi or laddus, and recite the Hanuman Chalisa. Saturday is the other day many families give to Hanuman.

### tuesday-vrat
- topic: weekday
- title: Mangalvar vrat customs
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: hanuman
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

The Mangalvar vrat is kept by many devotees for Hanuman: one meal a day, commonly without salt, often wheat or jaggery-based foods, with a visit to a Hanuman temple in the evening. Reciting the Hanuman Chalisa or Sundarkand is customary. Some keep twenty-one Tuesdays. In some families the fast is also linked to Mangal. Customs differ widely, and many people simply visit the temple without fasting.

### tuesday-design-idea
- topic: weekday
- title: Design ideas for Tuesday
- graha: mangal
- weekday: tuesday
- deity: hanuman
- source: Modern convention
- lang: en

For Tuesday, a Hanuman design follows the tradition: Hanuman with the gada (mace), Hanuman carrying the mountain, Hanuman revealing Ram in his heart, or a Hanuman Yantra. Red, orange and vermilion follow the commonly given colours. Designers might also use the Devanagari "Jai Shri Hanuman" or the Mangal beej mantra. These are style suggestions, not a ritual requirement, and Hanuman designs are equally suitable on Saturdays or any day.

### wednesday-overview
- topic: weekday
- title: Wednesday (Budhvar): Budh, Ganesha and Vishnu
- graha: budh
- weekday: wednesday
- deity: ganesha
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Wednesday (Budhvar) is the day of Budh, Mercury. By popular custom it is widely observed for Ganesha, the remover of obstacles and patron of learning, and some families also worship Vishnu or Krishna. Commonly worn colours are green. Devotees offer durva grass and modak to Ganesha. Observance varies by community.

### wednesday-vrat
- topic: weekday
- title: Budhvar vrat customs
- graha: budh
- weekday: wednesday
- deity: ganesha
- source: Regional practice (varies)
- lang: en

Some families keep a Wednesday fast (Budhvar vrat), often a single meal with green foods such as moong dal, and read the Budhvar Vrat Katha. Others simply visit a Ganesha temple. Green clothing or green offerings are customary in many homes. The Wednesday fast is less widely kept than Monday, Thursday or Saturday fasts, and practice depends on family and region.

### wednesday-design-idea
- topic: weekday
- title: Design ideas for Wednesday
- graha: budh
- weekday: wednesday
- deity: ganesha
- source: Modern convention
- lang: en

For Wednesday, a Ganesha design follows common custom: Ganesha with his modak, the mooshak (mouse) vahana, a single-line Ganesha, or a Ganesha Yantra. Green follows the commonly given colour for Budh, while red and orange are also popular for Ganesha. Designers might add "Om Gan Ganapataye Namah" in Devanagari. These are style suggestions, not a ritual requirement, and Ganesha designs suit any day.

### thursday-overview
- topic: weekday
- title: Thursday (Guruvar): Guru and Vishnu's day
- graha: guru
- weekday: thursday
- deity: vishnu
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Thursday (Guruvar or Brihaspativar) is the day of Guru, Jupiter, and is widely associated with Vishnu and Lakshmi-Narayan. It is also a day for honouring teachers; in many traditions it is observed for Dattatreya, and in modern popular practice many devotees visit temples of Sai Baba of Shirdi on Thursdays. Commonly worn colours are yellow and saffron. Practice varies a great deal between communities.

### thursday-vrat
- topic: weekday
- title: Brihaspativar vrat customs
- graha: guru
- weekday: thursday
- deity: vishnu
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

The Brihaspativar (Guruvar) vrat is kept by many families: one meal, commonly yellow foods such as chana dal and banana, worship of Vishnu or Brihaspati, and reading of the Brihaspati Vrat Katha. Devotees often wear yellow and offer yellow flowers. Some keep sixteen Thursdays. In many homes the banana tree is worshipped on this day. Customs vary, and the fast is a personal choice.

### thursday-design-idea
- topic: weekday
- title: Design ideas for Thursday
- graha: guru
- weekday: thursday
- deity: vishnu
- source: Modern convention
- lang: en

For Thursday, designs linked to Vishnu follow the tradition: the Sudarshan Chakra, shankh (conch), padma (lotus), Vishnu on Sheshnag, Lakshmi-Narayan together, or the Devanagari "Om Namo Narayanaya". Yellow, saffron and gold tones follow the commonly given colours. Designs of a guru or the Om can also suit a day for teachers. These are style suggestions, not a ritual requirement.

### friday-overview
- topic: weekday
- title: Friday (Shukravar): Lakshmi, Devi and Shukra
- graha: shukra
- weekday: friday
- deity: lakshmi
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Friday (Shukravar) is the day of Shukra, Venus, and is widely observed as the day of Lakshmi, the goddess of prosperity. In many regions it is also the day of Santoshi Maa, of Durga, and of other forms of the Goddess; Friday evenings are a favourite time for Devi temples. Commonly worn colours are white, cream or pastel, while red, pink and gold are popular for Lakshmi and Devi. Practice varies by region.

### friday-vrat
- topic: weekday
- title: Shukravar vrat and Santoshi Maa customs
- graha: shukra
- weekday: friday
- deity: santoshi maa
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

On Fridays many devotees worship Lakshmi, light a lamp in the evening and keep the home clean. The Santoshi Maa vrat, which became widely popular in the second half of the twentieth century, is kept on sixteen Fridays by many women, with jaggery and roasted gram (chana) as the prasad and a custom of avoiding sour foods. The Shukravar Vrat Katha is read. Customs differ by family and region.

### friday-design-idea
- topic: weekday
- title: Design ideas for Friday
- graha: shukra
- weekday: friday
- deity: lakshmi
- source: Modern convention
- lang: en

For Friday, designs linked to Lakshmi follow the tradition: Lakshmi seated on a lotus, the padma (lotus), the kalash with coconut, the Sri Yantra, or lamps (diyas). White, cream, pink, red and gold follow the commonly given or popular colours. A Santoshi Maa or Durga design also fits Fridays in many regions. These are style suggestions, not a ritual requirement.

### saturday-overview
- topic: weekday
- title: Saturday (Shanivar): Shani Dev and Hanuman
- graha: shani
- weekday: saturday
- deity: shani dev
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

Saturday (Shanivar) is the day of Shani, Saturn. Shani Dev is worshipped at dedicated temples, and in North India Hanuman is also worshipped on Saturdays; a well-known Puranic story, retold in popular tradition, links the two. In the South, Saturdays are also observed for Venkateswara (Balaji) in some families. Commonly worn colours are black and dark blue. Practice varies between regions.

### saturday-vrat
- topic: weekday
- title: Shanivar vrat customs
- graha: shani
- weekday: saturday
- deity: shani dev
- source: Common North Indian practice
- lang: en

The Shanivar vrat is kept by many devotees: one meal, often without salt, avoiding oil-fried food, and a visit to a Shani or Hanuman temple. Mustard or sesame oil, black sesame and black cloth are common offerings. Many give food or clothes to the needy. Reading the Hanuman Chalisa is customary on this day. Some keep seven or eleven Saturdays. Customs vary, and the fast is a personal choice.

### saturday-design-idea
- topic: weekday
- title: Design ideas for Saturday
- graha: shani
- weekday: saturday
- deity: shani dev
- source: Modern convention
- lang: en

For Saturday, designs linked to Shani Dev or Hanuman follow the tradition: Shani Dev on his crow or vulture, the Shani beej mantra in Devanagari, Hanuman with a gada, or a Hanuman Yantra. Black, dark blue and indigo follow the commonly given colours for the day. These are style suggestions based on custom, not a ritual requirement, and any deity design may be worn on any day.

### rahu-kaal-note
- topic: weekday
- title: Rahu Kaal, a daily period
- graha: rahu
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Regional practice (varies)
- lang: en

Rahu Kaal is a period of about ninety minutes each day, calculated from sunrise and sunset, and it falls at a different time on each weekday. In many regions, especially South India, families avoid starting new ventures or ceremonies during it, while worship and prayers are generally still done. Some almanacs (panchang) publish the timings daily. Not everyone observes it, and traditions differ about how strictly.

### vrat-general-note
- topic: weekday
- title: What a vrat is, and sensible observance
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

A vrat is a vow taken for devotion and self-discipline, often involving a fast, prayer and a katha (story) reading. Fasts range from complete abstinence to a single meal, fruits or milk, and vary by family. Tradition itself permits adjusting a fast for age, illness, pregnancy or work, and many teachers say sincerity and kindness matter more than strictness. Anyone with a health condition should follow medical advice first. A vrat is a devotional practice, not a transaction.
