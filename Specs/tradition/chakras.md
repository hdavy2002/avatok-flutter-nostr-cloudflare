# Chakras

### chakra-overview
- topic: chakra
- title: The seven chakras, an overview
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Shat-chakra-nirupana
- lang: en

In Tantric and yogic tradition the chakras (wheels or lotuses) are described as energy centres along the spine, usually counted as seven: Muladhara (root), Svadhisthana (sacral), Manipura (navel), Anahata (heart), Vishuddha (throat), Ajna (between the brows) and Sahasrara (crown). Each is linked to a lotus with a set number of petals, a bija (seed) sound and an element. The classical description most often cited is the Shat-chakra-nirupana, a sixteenth-century Tantric text. Different lineages describe the details differently.

### chakra-rainbow-colours-modern
- topic: colour
- title: The rainbow chakra colours are a modern convention
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Modern convention
- lang: en

The familiar scheme of red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo and violet for the seven chakras is a modern convention, popularised in the West during the twentieth century through Theosophical and New Age writing, and it is now common in yoga studios and wellness art. It is not found in the classical texts. The Shat-chakra-nirupana describes the lotuses and their regions in different colours. Pandit ji should therefore call the rainbow colours "the modern popular scheme", and never present them as ancient scripture.

- weekday:
- deity:

Translations of the Shat-chakra-nirupana, such as Arthur Avalon's "The Serpent Power", describe the lotuses in colours that do not match the rainbow scheme: for example a crimson Muladhara, a vermilion Svadhisthana, a dark, rain-cloud-coloured Manipura, a deep red Anahata, a smoky purple Vishuddha, a white Ajna and a shining white Sahasrara. Translators vary, and other Tantric texts give other schemes. These are described as meditation images, and a reviewer should check the wording against a trusted translation before use.

### chakra-muladhara
- topic: chakra
- title: Muladhara, the root chakra
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity: ganesha
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Muladhara ("root support") is traditionally placed at the base of the spine. Its element is earth (prithvi), its bija sound is LAM, and its lotus has four petals. The modern popular colour is red. In yogic tradition it is linked with stability, groundedness and the dormant Kundalini energy, which is said to rest here. In some traditions Ganesha is associated with this centre, and the Ganapati Atharvashirsha refers to him dwelling at the Muladhara. Descriptions vary by lineage.

### chakra-svadhisthana
- topic: chakra
- title: Svadhisthana, the sacral chakra
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Svadhisthana ("one's own abode") is traditionally placed at the root of the genitals or low in the abdomen. Its element is water (apas), its bija sound is VAM, and its lotus has six petals. The modern popular colour is orange. In yogic tradition it is linked with creativity, pleasure, flow and emotion. Systems differ about its exact location, and some texts give different deities for it. The colour stated here is the modern convention, not the classical one.

### chakra-manipura
- topic: chakra
- title: Manipura, the solar plexus chakra
- graha: surya
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Manipura ("city of jewels") is traditionally placed at the navel. Its element is fire (agni), its bija sound is RAM, and its lotus has ten petals. The modern popular colour is yellow. In yogic tradition it is linked with willpower, digestion, confidence and inner heat (tejas). Some modern writers connect it with the Sun, which matches the fire element, though that link is a popular association rather than a fixed rule. Lineages differ in their descriptions.

### chakra-anahata
- topic: chakra
- title: Anahata, the heart chakra
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Anahata ("unstruck") is traditionally placed at the centre of the chest, at the heart. Its element is air (vayu), its bija sound is YAM, and its lotus has twelve petals. The modern popular colour is green. The name refers to the "unstruck sound", an inner sound that arises without any two things striking. In yogic tradition it is linked with love, compassion and devotion (bhakti). Some traditions place the Ishta Devata, one's chosen deity, here for meditation.

### chakra-vishuddha
- topic: chakra
- title: Vishuddha, the throat chakra
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Vishuddha ("especially pure") is traditionally placed at the throat. Its element is ether or space (akasha), its bija sound is HAM, and its lotus has sixteen petals, linked in tradition with the sixteen Sanskrit vowels. The modern popular colour is blue, sometimes light blue. In yogic tradition it is linked with speech, truth and expression, and with chanting. The throat is also where Shiva is said to hold the Halahala poison as Neelkantha, though that is a Puranic story, not a chakra text.

### chakra-ajna
- topic: chakra
- title: Ajna, the third-eye chakra
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity: shiva
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Ajna ("command") is traditionally placed between the eyebrows, the spot of the tilak or bindi. Its bija sound is OM, and its lotus has two petals. It is not linked to one of the five elements in most descriptions, but to mind and awareness. The modern popular colour is indigo. In yogic tradition it is linked with insight and focus, and the third eye of Shiva is a related image in devotion. Texts differ about the details.

### chakra-sahasrara
- topic: chakra
- title: Sahasrara, the crown chakra
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity: shiva
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

Sahasrara ("thousand-petalled") is traditionally placed at the crown of the head or just above it. It is described as a lotus of a thousand petals and is linked with the highest state of union, often described as Shiva (consciousness) meeting Shakti (energy). It has no element, and its sound is Om or silence in many descriptions. The modern popular colour is violet or white. Practitioners describe it as a goal of meditation, and it is not an organ or something to be measured.

### chakra-bija-mantras
- topic: chakra
- title: The bija mantras of the seven chakras
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Widely followed tradition
- lang: en

The bija (seed) sounds commonly given are LAM for Muladhara, VAM for Svadhisthana, RAM for Manipura, YAM for Anahata, HAM for Vishuddha and OM for Ajna, with Sahasrara linked to Om or silence. In yoga classes they are chanted in sequence, each held at the relevant part of the body. The sounds come from Tantric texts and teachers, and different lineages teach different practices. Chanting is a contemplative practice, not a medical treatment.

### chakra-design-note
- topic: chakra
- title: Chakra T-shirt designs and what they depict
- graha:
- weekday:
- deity:
- source: Modern convention
- lang: en

Chakra T-shirt designs usually show seven lotuses or circles along a spine, in rainbow colours with a bija sound in Devanagari. Pandit ji can describe these as meditation imagery based on yogic tradition, with the rainbow colours as a modern style. A chakra design may be suggested to someone interested in yoga or meditation, and it is a symbol to wear, not a treatment. Using the classical colours or a single lotus is also a valid design choice.
