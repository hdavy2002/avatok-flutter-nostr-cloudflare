# `[CALL-MEDIA-540P-1]` — phone-first call media profile (2026-08-06)

Owner decision: clear video and clear audio on a handset, without paying HD
bandwidth. Client-only change. **No build triggered.**

## The profile

| | Wi-Fi / unknown | Cellular |
|---|---|---|
| Capture | 960x540 @ 30 | 640x360 @ 24 |
| Video sender cap | 850 kbps | 450 kbps |
| Degrade level 1 | 400 kbps | 250 kbps |
| Degrade level 2 | 150 kbps (video being switched off) | 150 kbps |
| Degradation preference | BALANCED (framerate gives way before the picture freezes) | same |
| Opus | mono, 40 kbps, `useinbandfec=1`, `usedtx=0` | same, QoS ladder 40 / 32 / 24 |
| Capture DSP | AEC + NS + AGC + high-pass | same |

**RED stays ON** (`callAudioRedExperimentV1`, prod KV `true`). It roughly
doubles the audio wire rate to ~80 kbps; the owner accepted that for the
investor MVP on 2026-08-06, on the grounds that intelligibility on a lossy
cellular link is worth more than the bytes.

Everything lives in `app/lib/core/audio_tuning.dart`:
`avaVideoConstraints`, `avaVideoMaxBitrateBps`, `avaAudioSenderCapBps`,
`avaMicConstraints`, `tuneOpusSdp`. There is deliberately no second copy.

## What the first pass got right

Shared 540p/360p capture constraints, the 40 kbps Opus cap, the lowered sender
ceilings, and Opus tuning on the SFU's publish offer and renegotiate answer —
the SFU peer connection previously inherited none of the P2P limits because it
is built after the general call setup.

## What the audit found still broken

1. **The video cap survived only until the first network hiccup.**
   `_applyVideoDegradeLevel(0)` — the RECOVERY rung — carried its own hard-coded
   `1200000`. Any congestion-then-recovery cycle handed the encoder 1.2 Mbps
   again for the rest of the call, and that rung also ignored cellular entirely.
   Both ceilings now come from `avaVideoMaxBitrateBps`.

2. **RED was silently off on the SFU path.** `enableRed: false` was hard-coded
   at the SFU call sites while P2P read `callAudioRedExperimentV1`, which is
   `true` in prod. Moving a call onto the SFU dropped the redundancy the flag
   exists to provide. Now passed in from the same flag — for the two PUBLISH
   offers only; the pull answer is recvonly and must not reorder payload types.

3. **RED was about to halve the primary audio stream.** `maxBitrate` on the
   sender bounds the WIRE, redundancy included. Capping at the 40 kbps Opus
   target with RED on squeezes the primary encoder to ~20 kbps — RED as a net
   quality *loss*. `avaAudioSenderCapBps` pads the cap by the redundancy factor.

4. **Mid-call camera-on published unbounded on the SFU.** The video sender does
   not exist until `publishVideo`'s `addTrack`, so the connect-time limits never
   touched it. Configuration now runs inside `publishVideo` *before*
   `createOffer` — codec preference has no effect if applied afterwards, since
   this transport never re-offers.

5. **Codec preference was gated on `config.video`** — what the call STARTED as —
   so audio calls that later turned the camera on fell back to libwebrtc's
   VP8-first order, which also left the L1T3 temporal layering inert. Gate
   dropped; the transceiver loop already filters to video. The P2P upgrade path
   now sets codec preference before its re-offer too.

6. **The Opus QoS ladder topped out at 48 kbps** — above the 40 kbps
   `maxaveragebitrate` in the fmtp line, so the top rung was unreachable and the
   telemetry reported upshifts that changed nothing. Rungs are now 40 / 32 / 24.

## Verification

No local Flutter toolchain (owner decision 2026-08-05) — CI is the compile net.
`python3 tool/check_design_guard.py --check all` green. Telemetry:
`call_qos_bitrate_changed` now carries `wire_cap_kbps` and `opus_red_active`
alongside `max_bitrate_kbps`, because with RED on those numbers differ and
reading either alone is misleading.

## Not done on purpose

No build triggered, no flag flipped, no worker deploy. The client half is inert
until the next build carries it.
