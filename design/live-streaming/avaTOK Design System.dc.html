01 · THE ETHOS

Six rules the page already keeps.

Named so the next page follows them deliberately instead of by imitation.

A
01

Type is the poster

Headlines are set in heavy condensed caps at 80–110px with a hard offset shadow. They are not labels on a layout — they are the layout. Everything else negotiates around them.

02

Ink, never light

Shadows are solid, zero-blur offsets in near-black — on text and on cards alike. Nothing glows, nothing blurs, nothing has a gradient. This is a press, not a screen.

₹
03

Price in the title

The rupee figure lives inside the headline, not in a corner badge — WALK & TALK WITH ROHAN · ₹129. Cost is part of the offer's name, so nobody has to hunt for it.

04

Torn, not cut

Where an image meets a card body, the seam is a deckle tear, not a straight rule. Collage art gets ripped edges over flat colour blocks. The page should feel assembled by hand.

क
05

Speak two languages

English carries the product; Hindi and Hinglish carry the feeling — Chai ho jaye?, Aapki awaaz, duniya tak. Never a translation of the same line. Always the aside that only a local would write.

1:1
06

Faces, not features

Every card leads with a real person in a real place — a Kolkata street, a Mumbai seafront. The product is people. Icons and diagrams stay out of the hero.

Contrast rule for card fillsTeal and brick take bone white display type; butter and oat take ink. Never cream-on-butter or ink-on-teal — the offset shadow stops reading and the whole card goes muddy.

The same pairing governs the 9px stub row, where it matters more because the type is tiny: on butter and oat the stubs and duration take #5A3D33 — the body colour, not the lighter label brown, which is a near-miss on oat. On teal and brick they go close to bone: #D6ECEE and #FBE4DF. Card prose on brick is plain #FDF1D3 — a tinted version drops under 4.5:1. Brick itself is #B8382A, not the lighter #C54131 sampled off the reference: at 9px the lighter fill leaves bone white a shade under AA.
03 · TYPOGRAPHY

Four voices.

A poster shout, a hand-painted signboard, a plain speaking voice and a ticket stub. Each one has exactly one job and they never swap.

Anton 400 · THE SHOUT

Every headline, card title, price and nav label. Always uppercase, tracking −0.015em. Leading is 1.0–1.08 — tighter than that and the offset shadow of one line lands on the cap-height of the next. On a coloured ground it always carries that shadow, so it never goes below 1.0.

05 · TRUCK ART GRAMMAR

Horn OK please.

The ornament layer. Everything above is the restrained press — cream, ink, one red. Truck art is what goes on top of it: the frames, the plaques, the dividers and the section headers. It is loud on purpose, so it is rationed on purpose.

The rule that keeps it from becoming a mela: one truck-art object per section. A framed plaque, or an ornate divider, or a decorated section header — never two, and never inside a creator card. The cards stay plain so the ornament reads as special.

THE TRUCK PALETTE — ORNAMENT ONLY, NEVER A PAGE GROUND
Cobalt#3A3A9E
Sky#00A8F0
Chrome#F0D018
Vermilion#D83030
Emerald#1E9E52 / #15773D
Magenta#F03060
Saffron#F0A830

Seven colours, used in threes. A truck-art object picks one dark (cobalt, emerald, vermilion), one bright (chrome, saffron, magenta) and white for the outlines — that triad is what makes the panels read as painted metal rather than as a colour test.

Emerald is a two-value token. #1E9E52 is the flat ornament green — fine for bands, frames and fields with no type on them. The moment text sits on it, drop to #15773D: bone white on the lighter green measures 3.1:1, on the darker one 5.0:1. Same principle as signal red in §02 — a colour sampled off painted metal is not automatically a colour you can set type on.

How the plaque is built

01Three nested frames — ink outer, bright middle, dark inner. Each ring 3px, each inset 7–9px. Never two rings, never four.
02Bead trim — a repeating radial-gradient dot row along the top and bottom inside edges. This single detail does most of the work.
03Outlined lettering — Anton caps in chrome yellow, -webkit-text-stroke in bone white with paint-order: stroke fill, then a hard drop shadow in the darkest ring colour.
04A Devanagari second line — Baloo 2 at 800. The Hindi is the joke or the blessing, never a translation of the English above it.
05A zigzag skirt — the sawtooth band closes the bottom, in the third colour of the triad.

Use it for the one line per page that is pure brand voice — a manifesto, a CTA header, an empty state. Never for a price or a form label.

PRIMITIVES — ALL PURE CSS, NO IMAGE ASSETS

Zigzag band

Two 135°/225° linear-gradients over a flat fill, 20px tile. The section divider and the skirt on every plaque. Height 18–24px.

Bead trim

A repeating radial-gradient dot row. 12px, 16px or 20px tile. Runs along the inside edge of a frame, or under a section header as a rule.

Scallop skirt

The awning edge — an inverted radial-gradient arc row, 32px tile. Sits at the bottom of a coloured band where a hard rule would feel too abrupt.

Awaz

Outline lettering

Fill, stroke, shadow — three colours, in that order. Only on display type over 32px, only inside a truck-art object. Prose never gets a stroke.

Devanagari display

Baloo 2 · 800 with a stepped two-colour shadow. One Hindi word or phrase per object. It carries feeling, never information a user must act on.

Handwriting

Mehrab arch

The cusped arch panel that holds the centre message. An asymmetric border-radius dome, a rotated square finial on top, and an inset ring in the third colour.

Extruded letters

A ten-step 1px-increment text-shadow stack in a darkened version of the fill — a solid side wall, not a blur. The signboard alternative to the outline treatment; pick one, never both.

Medallion roundel

Concentric rings via inset box-shadows, an octagonal inner field via clip-path, one motif at the centre. The flanking element on either side of an arch.

BORDER BANDS — THE STACK IS THE BORDER

A truck-art border is never one line. It is a stack of thin strips — a dot row, then a chequer, then the wide motif band, then the chequer and dot row again. Eight strip types cover every border on the references, and all eight are one CSS background declaration each. No image assets, no SVG.

Bead rowRADIAL-GRADIENT · 22PX TILE

The outermost strip on almost every reference. Always the first and last band in a stack.

ChequerREPEATING-LINEAR 90° · 18PX

The workhorse separator. Sits either side of a motif band to stop two patterns touching.

Tick rowREPEATING-LINEAR 90° · 13PX

A finer chequer. Use where the border is under 8px tall and squares would read as mush.

Diamond rowFOUR LINEAR-GRADIENTS · 20PX

The lozenge band off the truck cab panels. Reads as woven, so it can carry more height than a chequer.

SawtoothTWO LINEAR-GRADIENTS · 24PX

Triangles pointing down close a band; pointing up they open one. Only ever at the edge of a stack, never in the middle.

Fan scallopRADIAL-GRADIENT ARCS · 32PX

The awning. Softer than a sawtooth — use it when the band below carries faces or photography.

Candy stripeREPEATING-LINEAR 45° · 16PX

The barber twist that edges rose panels. One per stack — two diagonals in one border start to shimmer.

Petal rowOFFSET RADIAL ARCS · 26PX

Interlocking half-discs, top and bottom offset by half a tile. The cheapest way to fake a floral band.

MOTIF BAND · NEEDS ART
Motif bandREPEATING PNG / SVG TILE

Roses, lotuses, peacocks, the pair of eyes. The only band CSS cannot fake — it needs a real tile asset, drawn once and repeated.

THE STACK — THIN, THIN, WIDE, THIN, THIN
Desi dil se

Five band types, mirrored about the wide one: chequer 12px, bead 20px, candy 14px, motif 76px, then the same three back out. The stack is always symmetrical and always odd-numbered, so there is one unmistakable centre. Nothing above 5 strips per side — at 7 it stops reading as a border and starts competing with the content.

WHERE A BAND STACK GOES
Full-bleed section divider — the whole stack, edge to edge, between two sections. Page top and bottom — a 3-strip stack pinned above the nav and below the footer, framing the document. Plaque and frame edges — one or two strips, top and bottom of a truck-art object. Under a section header — a single bead or chequer strip in place of a hairline rule. Category tile tops — one strip per tile, band type varying by category.
AND WHERE IT DOES NOT
As a page or section background — bands are edges, never fields. Around a creator card — the cards stay plain, always. Two full stacks visible at once on the same screen. Behind or beside body copy, where the repeat fights the reading rhythm. On any interactive edge — inputs, dropdowns, tabs, table rules.
