The go-live sequence

In order. Each step is safe to stop at — you're never left half-migrated or half-switched-on.

  1. Deploy the worker to production

    One command. Ships all 22 commits: the money fixes, the listing pipeline, the card fixes, the sign-in fix. Changes no behaviour on its own — everything new is behind a switch.

    I run it · you approve

  2. Run six database migrations

    None run automatically, by design. Four are new; two have been sitting unapplied since the paid-sessions work started in August. Without them the paid lane refuses to start rather than misbehaving.

    I run them · you approve each

  3. Switch off the two identity gates

    identityGateContentActionsEnabled and listingPublishKycRequired, both to false. You can create and publish listings. Stranger-messaging protection stays on.

    Must be reversed before real money

  4. Make one listing yourself, end to end

    Create it, add a photo, publish it, and look at the card. This is the real test — not a green build. If the card shows a poster and a rupee price, the whole front half works, and two of the five bugs are proven fixed rather than assumed.

    You · 10 minutes

  5. Put a handful of real listings up, then switch /marketplace to real data

    Roughly six to ten, enough that a grid looks like a marketplace rather than an accident. Then the page swaps from the mockup to the live grid — one line, and the old page was never deleted. Doing this before there are listings just replaces a fake page with an empty one.

    You fill it · I flip it

  6. Turn the paid lane on — four flags together

    The two listings flags and the two checkout flags, in one go. Half-flipping them is now refused loudly instead of quietly opening two ways to charge for one seat.

    Only after step 4 passes

  7. Sell one ticket to a second person

    Two real people, two phones. A paid live event with more than one buyer is the only way to prove the fix that let just one person through the door. One is not a test.

    You + one other person

A Cashfree merchant account

Blocks UPI

Everything behind the Pay button is built: order creation, the signed webhook, escrow funding, ticket issue, refunds to the payer's UPI handle, affiliate payouts. The button itself needs their JavaScript kit, which needs an account.

Not Stripe: Stripe India needs a registered company, and avaTOK is an unregistered business. Cashfree onboards unregistered merchants.

An accountant, before you collect GST

Blocks charging tax

Listings now show 18% GST and the total, which is what you asked for. Nothing collects it — you have no GSTIN, and charging a tax you can't remit is much harder to unwind than not charging it.

Ask them: the rate, how it works across Indian states, and what a valid invoice must say. There's also an unresolved TDS question on creator payouts — settle both together or the books won't agree.

Deliberately dark

Built, switched off, and safe to leave that way. Nothing here degrades — each one refuses cleanly rather than half-working.

Cashfree UPI checkout

Off

Two flags off, and no credentials configured, so the routes return “unavailable” rather than attempting a payment.

Never run against a gateway — not even a sandbox. Every field in it comes from Cashfree's documentation, not a response anyone has seen. It needs a full sandbox run before a single rupee moves.

GST collection

Shown, not charged

The tax engine runs on every purchase and stores zeros while it's off, so the code path is exercised long before it touches anyone's money.

The important part: tax sits on top of the price, goes to its own account, and never enters the pot that gets split. Your creators earn exactly the same with tax on or off.
