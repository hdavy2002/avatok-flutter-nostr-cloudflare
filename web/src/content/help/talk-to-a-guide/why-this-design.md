## The short answer

Pandit ji suggests a design because **what is printed on it** is linked in Hindu tradition to the deity, day or planet that came up in your conversation. Each suggestion comes with its reason.

## How a design is matched

Pandit ji looks at each design in this order:

1. **Deity or sacred symbol on the design.** Shiva or a trishul links to Monday, Hanuman to Tuesday and Saturday, Ganesha to Wednesday, Vishnu to Thursday, Lakshmi to Friday, Surya to Sunday.
2. **Chakra, mandala or yantra on the design**, such as a Manipura chakra, a Sri Yantra or a Navagraha mandala. See [Chakras and mandalas](/help/talk-to-a-guide/chakras-and-mandalas).
3. **Colour of the print.** The main colours of the artwork: red links to Mangal (Tuesday), yellow to Guru (Thursday).
4. **Colour of the fabric.** Used last, and only to support the steps above.

### Example

> A **white** T-shirt with a **red Hanuman** design.
> - Deity: Hanuman, traditionally worshipped on **Tuesday** (and Saturday)
> - Print colour: red, Mangal, **Tuesday**
> - Fabric: white, neutral here
>
> Pandit ji might say: "Tradition links Hanuman ji and the colour red with Tuesday. You may like to wear this on Tuesdays."

## Where the reasons come from

Every design has a **tradition note**: which deity, symbol, chakra, day, colours, occasions and mantra it relates to. These notes come from our library of traditional sources and are **checked by our team before Pandit ji can use them**. He does not make up a link that is not in the note.

## A suggestion, not a requirement

You never have to buy anything to talk to him. Wearing a design is a personal act of devotion, not a guarantee of any result. See [Guidance, not guarantees](/help/talk-to-a-guide/guidance-not-guarantee).
