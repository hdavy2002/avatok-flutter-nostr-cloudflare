A strike is a warning. You get it when you break a rule on a call.

## The basics

- A strike is for **one call** and **one person**, a fan or an influencer.
- It is **active for 90 days**, then it expires.
- You get a message on WhatsApp and in the app. It says what happened, shows only **your own words**, and tells you how to appeal.
- After your **second** active strike, you get a warning that one more will pause your account.

## What breaks the rules

- abuse, threats or gaali
- sexual talk or adult services
- selling on a call (influencers can use the Shop instead)
- financial tips, loans or network marketing
- asking for or taking payment outside {{brand.name}}
- sharing or asking for phone numbers, UPI IDs or addresses

## How a strike is decided

Every call is recorded for safety, and an AI checks it after the call. If it is very sure, the strike can be given straight away. If it is less sure, our safety team looks first. Mentioning self-harm is never a strike.

## Three active strikes

Your account is **paused** while our team reviews it. Read [Why is my account paused?](/help/safety-and-privacy/why-is-my-account-paused).

## If you think it is wrong

You can [appeal each strike once](/help/safety-and-privacy/how-to-appeal-a-strike). If it is accepted, the strike is removed.

Example: Meena gets a strike on 1 November. It expires on 30 January. If she gets no more strikes, her record is clean again.
